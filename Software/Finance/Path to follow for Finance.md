# What I want you to do

Don't try to finish one book every week.

Instead:

Read 10–20 pages.

Highlight unfamiliar terms.

Then come to me.

For example:

> What is settlement?

or

> Why does Visa exist if banks already exist?

or

> Why do bonds exist?

or

> What is a yield curve?

We'll unpack it until it makes sense.

# One habit I'd add

Create a file called:

```
finance.md
```

Every new term goes in it.

Example:

```text
Liquidity

What is it?

Why does it exist?

Real-world example

Connections
```

Don't write dictionary definitions.

Write explanations in your own words.

If you can't explain it simply, that's usually a sign you haven't fully understood it yet.

---

I have one more suggestion, and I think it will make this journey much more rewarding.

Treat finance the way good engineers treat computer science.

A good engineer doesn't just know how to call an API—they understand why TCP exists, how operating systems schedule processes, and why databases use transactions.

Likewise, don't aim to become someone who knows which ETF is popular this year. Aim to become someone who understands **why banks exist, why exchanges exist, why brokers exist, why interest rates move, and why markets behave the way they do**.

If you do that over the next year, you'll have something much more valuable than a list of investment tips: you'll have a framework that lets you evaluate new ideas on your own.

And I'm happy to go through that journey with you, one concept at a time. I think it'll be one of the most valuable skills you build outside of software engineering.
