I actually think Obsidian is one of the best tools for this.

Because finance isn't a linear subject. It's a **knowledge graph**.

The goal isn't to memorize 500 definitions.

The goal is to eventually think:

> "Oh... a bond is related to interest rates, which are set by central banks, which affect inflation, which changes exchange rates, which affects multinational companies."

That's how experts think.

# One final suggestion

If we're going to spend the next year doing this, I wouldn't stop at building a **Finance Vault**.

I'd build a **Life Knowledge Vault** with finance as one pillar.

```text
Knowledge/

├── Finance/
├── Software Engineering/
├── Economics/
├── Mathematics/
├── Psychology/
├── Philosophy/
├── Health/
├── Communication/
├── Career/
├── Writing/
└── Decision Making/
```

That's because the biggest ideas overlap. Psychology explains investor behavior. Economics explains why markets move. Mathematics explains risk and probability. Software engineering helps you understand financial systems. Philosophy helps you decide what wealth is _for_.

And I think that's ultimately the goal you've been describing—not just to learn finance, but to develop a framework for making sound decisions throughout your life.
