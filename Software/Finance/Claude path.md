## The regulatory reality (read this first)

Nepal has real capital controls. The Foreign Exchange (Regulation) Act is Nepal's principal legislation governing foreign currency, cross-border payments, capital movement, and foreign investment, and grants NRB extensive power to control foreign currency flows. Nepal Rastra Bank has imposed capital controls prohibiting Nepalis from making foreign investments — the notice forbids purchasing equities abroad or transferring capital to foreign bank accounts.

In plain terms: **as a Nepali resident earning in NPR, you legally cannot wire money from a Nepali bank account into Interactive Brokers to buy US stocks/ETFs.** The only clearly legal paths into IBKR-style investing are:

- You have **foreign-earned income** (freelancing in USD, remote job paid in USD, etc.) — Nepali citizens studying or working abroad, or earning in USD, can legally fund accounts like Interactive Brokers via SWIFT, though direct outward remittance for "capital investment" from an NPR account still requires specific NRB approval.
- You're an **NRN (Non-Resident Nepali)** — NRNs can invest using foreign currency accounts.
- Small-scale via a **dollar prepaid card** (~$500/year limit) — many Nepali investors use the NRB Prepaid Dollar Card, applied for at banks like Nabil or Global IME using a PAN card, to fund micro-investing apps — this is a gray-ish but commonly used route, capped and small.

If none of that applies to you (pure NPR income, living in Nepal), IBKR isn't off the table forever — but it's not your legal next step _today_. I'm not a lawyer, so treat this as a starting point, not a final answer — worth a quick call to your bank's forex desk to confirm your specific situation.

## So what's actually your next step?

**1. Foundation check (5 minutes, no money needed)** Before adding anything, confirm: 3–6 months expenses saved separately from your SIP? If not, that's priority zero, not investing.

**2. Grow what you already have — nationally** This is 100% legal and open to you right now:

- Open a **Demat account + Meroshare + TMS** (if you haven't) — this is the gateway to NEPSE directly, not just SIP
- Apply for **IPOs** through Meroshare — historically decent returns for Nepali retail investors, low capital needed
- Look at **Nepali mutual funds** (closed-end funds like NIBLGF, NMB50, etc. trade right on NEPSE) — this gives you fund-style diversification you don't get from SIP alone, still 100% legal, no forex issue

**3. International — the legal route for you specifically**

- If you have _any_ USD income (even small freelance gigs) → that's your ticket to fund IBKR legally via SWIFT
- If not yet → the **dollar card + micro-investing app** route (Gotrade etc.) is the realistic small-scale entry point most Nepali retail investors actually use

**4. Order of operations** Emergency fund → keep SIP running → open Demat/Meroshare → try one IPO application → _then_ explore international once you have a legal funding source.

Here's the book-to-concept pairing, in the order that'll actually make international investing click — not just the books alone, but what to _extract_ from each one before moving to the next.

## Phase 1 — Mindset (1–2 weeks)

**Book:** _The Psychology of Money_ — Morgan Housel **Concepts to pull out:** compounding, risk tolerance, why behavior beats intelligence in investing, long-term thinking **Why first:** if your mindset isn't fixed, no amount of ETF knowledge saves you from panic-selling in a crash.

## Phase 2 — How markets actually work (2–3 weeks)

**Book:** _A Random Walk Down Wall Street_ — Burton Malkiel **Concepts to pull out:** efficient markets, why picking individual stocks is hard, market cycles, bubbles, why "random" doesn't mean "don't invest" **Pair with:** learn the mechanics — what NYSE/NASDAQ actually are, what a ticker is, market hours (US market runs evening in Nepal time), bid/ask spread

## Phase 3 — The ETF/Index bible (2–3 weeks)

**Book:** _The Little Book of Common Sense Investing_ — John Bogle **Concepts to pull out:** index funds vs active funds, expense ratios, why low cost compounds into huge differences over decades, what "beating the market" actually costs you **Pair with:** learn the specific tickers you'll actually consider — VOO/VTI (total US market), QQQ (tech-heavy Nasdaq), VXUS (international ex-US) — know what each one _actually holds_ before you touch it

## Phase 4 — Reading a company, if you ever go beyond ETFs (3–4 weeks)

**Book:** _One Up On Wall Street_ — Peter Lynch **Concepts to pull out:** how an ordinary person can understand a business, P/E ratio, growth vs value framing **Note:** you can genuinely skip this phase if you decide "I'm only ever doing ETFs" — that's a completely legitimate choice, not a lesser one.

## Phase 5 — Optional depth (only if 1–4 felt easy)

**Book:** _The Intelligent Investor_ — Benjamin Graham **Concepts to pull out:** margin of safety, Mr. Market allegory, value investing discipline

## Run in parallel with all phases — the non-book concepts

These aren't in any book above but matter just as much for you specifically:

- **Currency risk** — USD/NPR movement affects your real return even if the ETF itself goes up
- **Tax treaties & dividend withholding** — 30% US withholding on dividends for non-residents is standard, know it going in
- **NRB/forex rules** — from our last message: know exactly which legal funding route applies to you before you open any account
- **Broker mechanics** — order types (market vs limit), how SWIFT transfers actually move money, KYC requirements

## Realistic timeline

Books 1–3 alone (Housel → Malkiel → Bogle) is genuinely enough to invest responsibly in global ETFs. That's ~6-8 weeks of reading, done. Books 4–5 are for going deeper later, not blockers to starting.
